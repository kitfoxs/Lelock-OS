# Privacy

Lelock keeps its configuration, identity, local session history, operational journal and owned Palace under the chosen local home. Filesystem permissions are not encryption. A selected remote model receives supplied persona, conversation, relevant memories and selected file content. Provider storage/policies are outside this application's control. Local endpoints must also be examined for their own logging/network behavior.

Explicit memory is the default but does not disable local session history. Journal retention additionally archives direct turns/checkpoints in Palace. Temporary sessions use a separate home, no old-memory recall and no commits/writes, but do not guarantee crash cleanup, secure erasure, OS/provider zero retention or anonymity.

Only selected scope records are used. Separate personal/work/fiction homes; do not rely on tone switches for privacy. Datachip exports are selected plaintext, not whole backups. They exclude credentials, endpoint routing, raw transcripts and runtime data, but the exported memories themselves can be sensitive. Inspect before sharing.

Forgetting an active record does not erase every copy in transcripts, logs, backups or remote systems. No telemetry/analytics service is added by Lelock's code, but dependencies and selected providers must be reviewed separately. Full operational details and known limits are in the enclosing blueprint.
